Aqiqah vs Qurban:
Key Differences Explained

Both involve slaughtering a halal animal. Both are acts of worship. But aqiqah and qurban are not the same. Here is the clear breakdown you need before you book.

Many families ask us: "Can I combine aqiqah and qurban into one slaughter?" The short answer is no. They serve different purposes, have different timings, and carry different rulings. Understanding the difference helps you fulfil both obligations correctly.

Aqiqah: Celebrating a New Life

Aqiqah comes from the Arabic word meaning "to cut." It refers to the practice of slaughtering a goat to mark the birth of a child, alongside cutting the baby's hair and giving the child a name. It is rooted in this hadith:

"Every child is pledged to his aqiqah, slaughtered on his behalf on the seventh day, his head is shaved and he is given a name."

(HR. Abu Dawud, Tirmizi, Nasa'i, Ibn Majah — Sahih)

Key points about aqiqah: the animal is slaughtered in the name of the child, not the parent. Two goats are preferred for a boy, one for a girl. If you missed aqiqah at birth, you can still perform it today, for yourself or your child.

Qurban: The Sacrifice of Eid al-Adha

Qurban commemorates the willingness of Prophet Ibrahim (AS) to sacrifice his son Ismail as an act of obedience to Allah. Allah replaced the sacrifice with a ram at the last moment. Muslims perform qurban every Eid al-Adha in remembrance of this.

"It is neither their meat nor their blood that reaches Allah, but it is piety from you that reaches Him."

(Quran 22:37)

Qurban must be performed during the Days of Tashriq (10th to 12th Zulhijjah). You cannot perform it on any other date. The meat is divided into three equal parts.

Can Aqiqah and Qurban Be Combined?

The majority position: No, they cannot be combined.

Each act requires its own animal and its own niyyah (intention). Aqiqah is for the child. Qurban is for yourself. They are performed at different times and for different reasons. Performing one does not fulfil the other. This is the position of the four major madhabs (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) and the majority of contemporary scholars.

If your child was born around Eid al-Adha and you wish to perform both aqiqah and qurban, you need two separate animals: one for your qurban, and one (or two) for the aqiqah.

Aqiqah vs Qurban: FAQ

Aqiqah is performed to celebrate the birth of a child. Qurban is performed during Eid al-Adha as an act of worship. Both involve halal slaughter but they differ in purpose, timing, who they are for, and the rules around meat distribution. They cannot be combined into a single slaughter.

No. The majority of Islamic scholars hold that aqiqah and qurban serve different intentions and cannot be combined. Each requires a separate animal and a separate niyyah. If a child is born around Eid al-Adha, you need one animal for your qurban and a separate animal for the aqiqah.

Aqiqah: 2 goats for a boy, 1 goat for a girl (1 goat for a boy is acceptable if 2 is not feasible). Qurban: 1 goat per person, or share 1 cow among up to 7 people.

Aqiqah is ideally performed on the 7th day after birth. If not possible, the 14th or 21st day is recommended. After that, aqiqah can be performed at any point in life, including adulthood (known as aqiqah dewasa).

Qurban is performed on the 10th, 11th, or 12th of Zulhijjah during Eid al-Adha. It cannot be performed outside of these three days. Book your slot at Bani Ibrahim Farms early as slots fill up fast.

Aqiqah is recommended for parents on behalf of their newborn child. If parents did not perform it, the child may perform it for themselves as an adult. This is valid and is accepted by the majority of scholars.
